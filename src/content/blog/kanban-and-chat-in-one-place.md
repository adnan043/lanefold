A board answers "what are we doing, and who has it?" Chat answers "what do you think about this?" Both questions come up on the same piece of work, so it helps when they are one click apart.

## Keep decisions on the card

Chat is fast and forgettable. A card is slower and lasting. A good habit: talk in chat, then write the decision on the card. A one-line comment such as "Going with option B, ship Thursday" saves the next person from reading a long thread.

## Give messages a card to point at

When someone says "we should fix the export bug" in chat, do not trust that someone will remember. Create a card for it on the board so the work has a lane, an owner and a place to be tracked, then attach that card to your message with #. Everyone who reads the message can open the card from it.

## Share a card into a conversation

When you want opinions on a specific card, attach it to a message with # instead of describing it. Everyone sees the same thing.

## Pick the right place to talk

- **Team channels** for general questions and announcements.
- **Board chat** for the work on one board, so people who care about that board see it.
- **The card** for anything you want to find again in a month.

## Keep the noise down

- Mute channels you only need occasionally.
- Use the Inbox to see what needs you, instead of watching every channel.
- Prefer one message with the full question over five short ones.

Chat should shorten the path to a decision. The board is where the decision ends up.
